The phone app shows what is happening in Tori on your Mac and lets you act
on it while you are away from the desk. It is a window onto the Mac, not a
second Tori: the agents keep running on the Mac, and the phone talks to it
over Tailscale. Pair it first, see
[Pairing and remote access](/docs/phone/pairing/).

## Find a session

The app opens on your projects.

- The space switcher along the bottom picks a space, like the tiles on the
  Mac. Each space shows its status bubble.
- **Projects** lists the space's projects, each with its status rollup and how
  many sessions are running.
- **Topics**, in the bottom bar, lists your [Topics](/docs/workspace/topics/)
  with their members.
- The search button searches **Projects, worktrees, sessions** at once.

Tap a project for its worktrees. Each worktree card shows its branch, the
lines changed, its commits ahead and behind, its pull request and checks, and
an issue chip, the same facts the desktop sidebar shows. A worktree the
autopilot is working in is tinted violet.

A git project also has **Fan out**, the same as on the Mac: say what the
attempts are for, pick **2**, **3** or **4**, and Tori makes that many
attempts, each a worktree on its own branch named from the goal. Promoting one
attempt deletes the others, so **Promote this attempt** stays on the Mac. See
[Fan out](/docs/git/worktrees-and-branches/#fan-out-several-attempts).

In a project that shows branches rather than worktrees, **Attach a branch**
under the list shows an existing local branch there, the same as picking a
local branch in the Mac's add branch dialog. Type its name and tap
**Attach**.

## Spaces and Topics

Tap **Spaces** next to the space's name for the **Spaces** sheet:

- The arrows move a space up or down, in the order the Mac shows too.
- **Edit** sets a space's colour (**Auto** derives it from the name) and icon
  (the first tile wears the space's initials).
- **New space** makes a folder of that name in Tori's base folder, with the
  colour and icon you pick.

Deleting a space removes its folder and everything in it, so it stays on the
Mac. See [Spaces](/docs/workspace/spaces/).

The **Topics** tab ends with **New Topic**: a name, a branch, and the
repositories in it, each **Worktree** (its own worktree on the branch) or
**Reference** (read only, the repository's own checkout). The branch is
needed only when a repository is a worktree.

A Topic's **More** has:

- **Rename**.
- **Add a repository**: one of your projects not in the Topic yet, as a
  worktree or a reference.
- **Promote a member**: a reference member gets its own worktree on the Topic
  branch.
- **Move a member up**: one place, in the order the Mac shows too.
- **Delete Topic**: only the Topic goes; its worktrees and branches stay.

A worktree's **More** has **Move to Topic**, which adds the worktree to a
Topic as it is, on its own branch. See [Topics](/docs/workspace/topics/).

## A worktree

A worktree shows its branch and standing, then its sessions in two groups:
**Now**, what is running, and the rest as history, grouped by when they were
last active, the same sessions as the Mac's History dropdown. Tap a session to
open it. An ended chat opens with its transcript and a box that reads
**Message to resume**: what you send there resumes it, on the Mac too.

**More** (the three dots) has **Move to Topic** and **Remove worktree**, for a
worktree in a project. The phone asks **Remove worktree "name"?** first. The branch stays,
and so does everything committed on it. The phone never forces the removal:
a worktree with uncommitted changes, or with a session still running in it, is
refused with the reason, so commit, or close the session, first. Deleting the
branch with it stays on the Mac.

**New** opens the **New session** sheet. Pick where the session runs, then
tap **Start**:

- **Here**: in this worktree.
- **New worktree**: on a new **Branch**, made **From** the ref you type, or
  the default branch when **From** is empty. The project's setup command runs
  in it first, as on the Mac.
- **From an issue**: pick one of the issues assigned to you. Tori makes a
  worktree on the issue's suggested branch, the worktree remembers the issue,
  and an empty **First message** sends the issue's title and body.
- **Review a PR**: pick one of the pull requests waiting on your review. Tori
  makes a worktree on its head, forks included.

**New worktree**, **From an issue** and **Review a PR** show only for a
worktree that belongs to a project.

The sheet also has:

- **Agent**: the agents that run as a chat, starting on the one last used in
  this worktree.
- **Account**: shown when the agent has more than one account, with each
  account's email when Tori knows it.
- **First message**: optional; the session starts and waits when it is empty.
- **Run in background**: starts the session as background work. At the
  default execution level it runs boxed, in the agent's own sandbox. See
  [Unattended sessions](/docs/agents/unattended/).

A session you start in the foreground counts as watched while the app is
open, so at the default level it is not boxed even with the Mac's lid
closed. Pick its model and mode in the chat once it opens.

## Read and steer a chat

A chat looks the way it does on the Mac: prompts, replies, tool calls, diffs.
Scroll up and earlier history loads in pages.

- **Send a message.** While a turn runs, the box reads **Steer the running
  turn**: what you send goes into that turn, as a steer. Otherwise it starts the
  next turn.
- **Switch model, effort or mode** from the pickers in the box. Effort shows
  for a model that lists effort levels. A switch applies from the next turn.
- **The agent's own options**, when it has any, sit beside them: a picker for a
  choice, a chip that turns on or off for a switch. They are the same options
  the Mac shows for that agent.
- **Slash commands.** Type `/` and the agent's commands show above the box,
  narrowing as you type. Tap one to put it in the box.
- **The plan card** shows above the box while the agent has a plan, the same
  steps the Mac shows.
- **Context and cost** show in one line above the box: the context used out of
  the model's window, with its share, and what the session has cost so far.
- **More** has **Stop** (stop the running turn), **Rename**, **Close
  session**, **Changes**, **Send to a new chat**, **Fork this chat**, **Copy
  session id** and **Open worktree**.
- **Rename** names the session on the Mac too. Leave the name empty to go back
  to its title.
- **Close session** ends a running chat, as closing its tab on the Mac does.
  It moves to the worktree's history, and a message there resumes it.
- **Send to a new chat** starts a new chat in the same folder, on the same
  agent, with what is in the box (and its attachments) as its first message.
- **Fork this chat** starts a new session that carries this conversation on,
  on the same agent. It shows once the chat has a turn.

### Changes and reverts

**More > Changes** lists the session's turns that changed files, each with how
many files. **All changes** at the top is everything the session changed, as
one diff. Tap a turn for that turn's diff.

**Rewind** on a turn puts the worktree's files back to how they were before
that turn: what the session wrote in it and in every turn after it is undone.
The phone asks **Rewind the files to before turn N?** first. Tori takes a
backstop before rewinding, so the Mac's checkpoint timeline can bring the
files back. Unlike a rewind on the Mac, the conversation is not cut: it goes
on as it was, and the agent still remembers writing what was undone. A rewind
is refused while another live session works in the same folder.

A tool call that edited files opens to its diff, the same as on the Mac.
**Revert** on one hunk asks **Revert this hunk of name?** and puts only those
lines back.

### Attach photos and files

The paperclip next to the pickers attaches files to your next message:

- **Photo library**: one or more photos or images from the phone.
- **Camera**: take a photo now.
- **File**: one or more files from the phone's file picker.

Each file uploads to your Mac as soon as you pick it, into
`~/.config/tori/attachments/`, the same place the Mac's composer writes to.
Its chip reads **Uploading** until the upload finishes, then shows its label
and name; the **x** removes it. Send waits until every upload has finished, and
a message can be attachments alone.

Labels are numbered by kind within the message, `[Image 1]`, `[PDF 1]`,
`[File 1]`, and Tori adds them at the end of your text, so you can write
"compare `[Image 1]` with `[Image 2]`". The agent reads each file from its path
on the Mac.

Limits:

- A photo larger than 2048 pixels on its long edge is scaled down on the phone
  and sent as a JPEG, so camera photos fit.
- The phone sends files up to 8 MB; a larger one is refused with its size.
  Images and other files are also held to the Mac's 5 MB limit.
- Up to 10 attachments per message, and the same kinds the Mac refuses (video,
  audio, archives, Office documents) are refused here.
- Claude takes images, PDFs and any other file. The other agents take files
  only, and refuse an image or a PDF when you send.

See [Attach files and images](/docs/agents/composer/#attach-files-and-images)
for the Mac's side.

## Answer what is waiting

Whatever a session is waiting on shows as a card at the bottom of its chat,
and the session's dot turns rose in the lists:

- **A permission prompt**: **Allow Bash?** with the arguments, and **Allow
  once** or **Deny**.
- **A question**: its options as buttons, or **Or type an answer**.
- **An approval** from the autopilot: the draft, **Show the draft** to read all
  of it, and **Approve** or the other answers.
- **A worker's question**: marked **A worker asks**.

Answering from the phone is the same as answering on the Mac; the card closes on
both. To allow one command for every worker asking for it, use the Inbox's
**Permissions** tab instead.

## The Inbox

The tray button in the bottom bar opens the Inbox, with the same four tabs as
on the Mac, the first one labelled **Since**. The button counts the Needs you rows you have not answered plus the
sessions waiting for review. See [The Inbox](/docs/git/inbox/).

**Since you looked** lists what happened since you last looked: finished
sessions with their lines and verdict, pull requests, usage limits and what
still waits on you. The Inbox opens on it when it has something. It counts as
seen when you leave the tab or put the app in the background, and the Mac then
starts from the same point. See
[Since you looked](/docs/git/inbox/#since-you-looked).

**Needs you** lists what needed you in the last day, grouped by project, with
answered rows dimmed. Tap a row to open its chat, where a waiting approval or
question sits above the message box; a pull request row opens its worktree.
Opening a chat on the phone, from anywhere, answers its **Finished** rows and
its branch's pull request rows, on the Mac too.

**Permissions** lists every permission prompt waiting right now, grouped by
project and then by the command asked, the same as on the Mac. **Always allow
for all** and a number answers the whole group with **Always in this
project**, **Deny** on a row denies that one prompt, and tapping a row opens
its chat. See [Permissions](/docs/git/inbox/#permissions).

**Review** lists sessions with work you have not reviewed yet, riskiest first.
Each card shows the score, the session's name, folder and agent, its last reply,
and the signals behind the score.

- **Approve** marks the session reviewed, the same as **Mark reviewed** on the
  Mac, and the row leaves both lists. While the session is still working the
  button reads **Still working** and does nothing.
- **Reply** opens the session's chat to send it a message. It shows only while
  the session is live; an ended session has no Reply.

## The autopilot

The wheel in the bottom bar opens the autopilot, with the same time of day scene
as the Cockpit:

- **Set sail** and **Drop anchor** start and stop it.
- **Chat** is its own conversation, with **Message the autopilot** to hand it
  work.
- Decision cards for anything waiting on you.
- **Crew**, the workers running now, and **Log**, what happened.

See [Autopilot](/docs/automation/autopilot/).

## Alerts with the app closed

The app only hears what happens while it is open and connected. To hear about
a session that needs you with the app closed, turn on **Settings > Alerts when
closed**. The phone asks for permission to show notifications the first time.

When a session needs you or finishes, the phone shows a notification with the
project's name and what happened:

- **A session needs approval**, **A session has a question**, **A session is
  waiting**, **A session hit its budget**, **A session had a command
  blocked**, or **A pull request needs a look**.
- **A session finished** when a turn ends.

Tap it and the app opens that session's chat, saying **Opening a session in**
and the project's name while it connects. Answer the session on the Mac
instead, and the notification goes away on the phone by itself. A session
that alerts again replaces its earlier notification rather than adding one.

The Mac sends nothing for:

- a session you are looking at on the Mac, with its window focused;
- a worker whose question reaches you through the chat that spawned it, or
  through the autopilot;
- anything at all while **Remote** is off in the Mac's Settings.

The Mac's own notification switches do not change what the phone gets. An
alert the phone cannot receive within an hour, because it is off or offline,
is dropped.

**Disconnect** in Settings turns alerts off on the phone. Removing the phone
in Settings > Remote on the Mac stops them as well.

### What leaves your Mac

Alerts travel through Google's push service (FCM) and a small relay Tori runs
at `relay.gettori.app`, because only a push service can wake an Android app
that is closed. Each alert is encrypted on the Mac with a key the phone made
and sent to the Mac when you turned alerts on, so neither Google nor the
relay can read it. The relay stores nothing.

- **The relay sees** your Mac's IP address, when an alert is sent, and how
  large it is.
- **Google sees** your phone's push token and when an alert is sent.
- **Neither sees** the project, the session, what it needs you for, or any
  path. Those are inside the encrypted alert, and only the phone opens it.

### When the switch stays off

The switch turns back off and says why when alerts cannot work:

- **Notifications are off for Tori. Turn them on in Android settings.** You
  declined the permission. Allow notifications for Tori in Android's settings,
  then turn the switch on again.
- **This phone has no working Google Play services, which alerts with the app
  closed need.** Phones without Google's services, such as many de-Googled
  ROMs, cannot get alerts with the app closed.
- **Tori on your Mac is too old for alerts with the app closed: update Tori**.
- **Connect to Tori first.** Turning alerts on needs the phone connected to
  the Mac.
- **This build of Tori has no push setup.** An app built from source without
  Tori's Firebase config cannot get alerts. The release APK has it.

## Settings

The gear opens a sheet:

- **Connection**: the Mac's address, this phone's name, the status, and
  **Disconnect**, which forgets the Mac on the phone. Revoke the phone in
  Settings > Remote on the Mac too, to remove it there.
- **Alerts when closed**: notifications on this phone while the app is
  closed. See [Alerts with the app closed](#alerts-with-the-app-closed).
- **Appearance**: **Tori Dark** or **Tori Light**, kept on this phone.
- **Autopilot button**: whether the wheel shows in the bottom bar.

If the Mac revokes the phone, the app says **This phone was removed from Tori.
Pair it again to reconnect.**

## When the app and Tori disagree

Tori on the Mac updates itself, but the app updates only when you install a
newer APK. When the two no longer speak the same protocol, the app says which
one to update in a banner instead of failing:

- **Tori on your Mac is too old for this app: update Tori**. Update Tori on the
  Mac, from the update notice in its title bar.
- **This app is too old for Tori on your Mac: update the app**. Install the
  newer APK from the
  [releases page](https://github.com/gettori/tori/releases). A release that
  needs a newer app says so in its notes.

## What the phone cannot do

The phone can read everything, steer, stop, answer, approve, review finished
work, start a session here, in a new worktree, from an issue or as a pull
request review, in the foreground or the background, attach photos and files,
switch a chat's model and mode, rename, close and resume sessions, remove a
worktree, attach a branch, make, edit and reorder spaces, make and change
Topics, fan out, fork a chat, read and revert a session's changes, and start or
stop the autopilot. It cannot delete a session or
a space, promote an attempt, delete or check out a branch, or pair another
device. Those stay on the Mac.

## Platform

The app is for Android, as an APK on the
[releases page](https://github.com/gettori/tori/releases), not through the Play
Store. See [Install](/docs/install/). An iOS app is planned.
